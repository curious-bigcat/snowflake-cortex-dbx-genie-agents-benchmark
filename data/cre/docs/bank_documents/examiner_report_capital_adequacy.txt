OFFICE OF THE COMPTROLLER OF THE CURRENCY
EXAMINER'S REPORT — CAPITAL ADEQUACY REVIEW

Bank: Pacific Northwest Bank, Portland, Oregon
Charter Number: 14892
Examination Date: September 2023
Examiner-in-Charge: Jonathan Blake, National Bank Examiner

CAPITAL COMPONENT RATING: 3 (Less Than Satisfactory)

1. CAPITAL ADEQUACY ASSESSMENT

Pacific Northwest Bank's capital position has deteriorated significantly over the past
two years, primarily driven by losses and provisions related to the CRE portfolio.

Capital Ratio Trends:
                 YE 2021    YE 2022    Q3 2023    Direction
CET1 Ratio       11.4%      10.2%      8.1%       Declining
Tier 1 Ratio     12.8%      11.5%      9.4%       Declining
Total Capital    15.2%      14.0%      11.8%      Declining
Leverage Ratio   9.8%       8.8%       7.3%       Declining

The 330 basis point decline in CET1 from YE 2021 to Q3 2023 is alarming and reflects:
- Cumulative net charge-offs of $97M (2021-2023)
- Additional provision expense of $95M in 2023
- Dividend payments of $56M in 2022 and $42M in 2023 (prior to suspension)
- Continued RWA growth as CRE portfolio expanded

2. STRESS TESTING CONCERNS

The Bank's internal stress test projects CET1 declining to 6.1% under severe adverse
conditions, which is BELOW the 7.0% well-capitalized threshold. This means:

(a) The Bank would be classified as "adequately capitalized" under stress,
    triggering restrictions on brokered deposits and certain other activities
(b) If actual losses exceed stress projections, the Bank could approach the
    "undercapitalized" threshold (4.5% CET1), which would trigger Prompt
    Corrective Action (PCA) requirements

3. DIVIDEND POLICY

The Bank paid common dividends totaling $98M during 2022-2023 despite deteriorating
capital ratios. The payout ratio reached 62% in Q2 2023. This was imprudent given:
- Rising CRE concentrations and deteriorating credit quality
- Capital ratios trending toward minimums
- Known OCC examination concerns communicated in prior supervisory letters

The dividend suspension ordered in the Consent Order is appropriate and should have
been implemented by management no later than Q1 2023.

4. CAPITAL PLAN ASSESSMENT

The Bank's Capital Plan (submitted February 2024) proposes:
- $175M subordinated debt issuance
- $412M CRE loan sale
- $200M RWA optimization
- Dividend suspension

We assess this plan as MINIMALLY ADEQUATE. The actions, if executed on time, should
restore CET1 above well-capitalized thresholds. However:
- The plan relies heavily on market execution (sub debt pricing, loan sale completion)
- The stress buffer (80 bps above 7.0% under severe stress) is thin
- No equity issuance is planned unless conditions deteriorate further

Recommendation: The Bank should prepare a shelf registration for common equity issuance
($100-150M) as a contingency measure.

5. CONCLUSIONS

The capital component is rated 3 (Less Than Satisfactory) due to:
- Significant capital deterioration over a short period
- Insufficient stress buffers
- Delayed management action on dividends
- Reliance on market conditions for capital restoration
