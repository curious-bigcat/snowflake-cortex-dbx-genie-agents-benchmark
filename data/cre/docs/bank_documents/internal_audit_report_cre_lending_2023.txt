1. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

This audit reviewed CRE origination practices during the Bank's period of rapid growth
(2021-2022). We sampled 1,200 of the 5,847 CRE loans originated during the audit period
(20.5% sample rate).

OVERALL RATING: UNSATISFACTORY

Key Findings:
- 23% of sampled CRE originations had one or more underwriting exceptions
- Exception rate was highest at Portland Main (BR-001) at 31% and Seattle Central (BR-004) at 28%
- Boise Downtown (BR-017) had the lowest exception rate at 12%
- Most common exception types: DSCR below policy (42% of exceptions), LTV above policy (28%),
  missing Environmental Phase I (18%), missing Credit Committee approval (12%)

2. DETAILED FINDINGS

Finding IA-2023-017-01 (HIGH): DSCR Exception Rate
- 276 of 1,200 sampled loans (23%) had one or more exceptions
- Of these, 116 loans had DSCR below the 1.25x policy minimum
- Average DSCR for exception loans: 1.12x (vs 1.25x policy minimum)
- 18 loans had DSCR below 1.00x at origination (negative leverage)
- Exception approvals frequently lacked documented compensating factors

Finding IA-2023-017-02 (HIGH): LTV Exception Rate
- 77 loans had LTV above the 75% policy maximum at origination
- Average LTV for exception loans: 79.3%
- 12 loans had LTV above 85% at origination
- Single-officer approvals used for 34 of these exceptions ($892M aggregate)
  when Credit Committee approval was required

Finding IA-2023-017-03 (HIGH): Missing Environmental Reports
- 50 loans (approximately 15% of CRE originations above $5M) were missing
  required Phase I Environmental Site Assessment reports
- 8 properties were subsequently identified with potential environmental concerns
  that would have been flagged by Phase I assessment

Finding IA-2023-017-04 (MEDIUM): Credit Committee Bypass
- 47 loans exceeding the $10M Credit Committee threshold were approved by
  individual officers or regional committees without full Credit Committee review
- Aggregate exposure of these loans: $1.8 billion
- This finding was concentrated in Q4 2021 and Q1 2022 when origination volume
  peaked and committee meeting frequency did not increase

Finding IA-2023-017-05 (MEDIUM): Appraisal Age
- 23 properties with appraisals older than 18 months were not flagged for update
  as required by the Bank's Appraisal Policy Section 4.3
- Combined exposure: $412M
- 7 of these properties have since been reappraised with values 20-40% below
  the stale appraisal used for ongoing covenant testing

4. ROOT CAUSE ANALYSIS

The high exception rate is attributable to:
(a) Aggressive growth targets: CRE origination targets increased 85% from 2020 to 2021
    without corresponding increase in underwriting staff or committee capacity
(b) Incentive misalignment: Loan officer compensation was primarily volume-based with
    insufficient quality metrics
(c) Insufficient oversight: Credit Committee meeting frequency (monthly) was inadequate
    for the volume of loans requiring review
(d) Technology gaps: Exception tracking was manual (spreadsheet-based) with no
    automated policy limit checks in the loan origination system

5. RECOMMENDATIONS

(a) Implement automated underwriting exception detection in loan origination system
(b) Revise loan officer compensation to include credit quality metrics
(c) Increase Credit Committee meeting frequency to bi-weekly
(d) Hire 15 additional credit analysts to support underwriting capacity
(e) Conduct portfolio-wide re-underwriting of all 2021-2022 CRE originations
(f) Update Environmental Policy to require Phase I prior to commitment letter issuance
